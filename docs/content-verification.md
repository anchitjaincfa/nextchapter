# Content Verification

Relationship prompts can cause harm even without AI. Every prompt has an owner, source/provenance, intended audience, locale, sensitivity, review date, evidence note, and immutable version.

## Lifecycle

Draft → editorial review → safety/clinical review where applicable → accessibility and inclusive-language review → legal/privacy review if claims exist → approved → published → monitored → revised or retired.

Two reviewers are required for high-sensitivity topics. Reviewers assess neutrality, reading level, cultural assumptions, coercion risk, false authority, answer symmetry, accessibility, and whether a user could mistake copy for diagnosis, treatment, prediction, crisis response, or legal advice.

## Prohibited content

No diagnosis, compatibility score, manipulation tactic, guaranteed outcome, blame assignment, instructions to confront an unsafe partner, or claim that the product can identify abuse. Avoid forced disclosure and open text in the demo. Crisis copy says the product is not monitored and directs immediate danger to local emergency services without presenting the product as a hotline.

Changes create a new content version; submissions remain bound to the version shown. Translation is separately reviewed, not machine-published. Feedback never automatically changes content.

## Release evidence

Maintain reviewer identities/roles, decision, rationale, date, next review, source links, localization status, and known limitations. Metrics must be aggregate and privacy-preserving; never optimize for more disclosure or reveal completion.
